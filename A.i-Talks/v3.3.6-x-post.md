## Why this version

- **Security leads, not the commit count.** "We found a thing that exposed your
  data and fixed it" earns trust; "63 commits" is supporting evidence. The
  numbers are in post 1 as backing, then it moves straight to what it means for
  the reader.
- **Post 4 is the one that actually differentiates you.** Publishing "our own
  burn total was 15M out of date" is what nobody else does. It costs nothing —
  the error understated your own work — and it's the strongest possible proof
  that the transparency claim isn't decoration. Do not soften it.
- **Post 5 is the real thesis.** Anyone can fix a bug. The tests are the story:
  the numbers are now tied to the source they should have come from.
- Post 3 explicitly says the hand-off still works, so nobody thinks a feature
  was taken away.

## Before you hit post

- **The governance line was PULLED from post 4 on purpose — do not add it back
  yet.** It is an open product question, not a settled correction. Until
  2026-07-17 the voting bar was 1 WinLEW, so "verify and you get a free vote"
  was effectively true and worked as an incentive to verify. Raising the bar to
  1,000,000 made that copy false as a side effect. Announcing it now would
  advertise the loss of an incentive you may want to restore — and if you do
  restore it, the post would be wrong. Decide first, then say something.

- **Don't say "audited" in a way that implies a third-party firm.** This was an
  internal review. If someone asks, say so plainly — it's still a strong claim.
- **No evidence either issue was exploited**, and both were found and fixed in
  the same pass. Worth saying if asked; don't claim it proactively, because
  "no evidence of exploitation" isn't "it didn't happen."
- If you attach a link, use **`/transparency/`** — it's the page where every
  figure was verified on-chain, so it backs the thread's whole argument.
- Best posting window is when your Discord is awake; the thread rewards replies
  and post 4 will draw questions.
