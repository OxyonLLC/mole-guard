Someone Minted A Fake $WinLEW. We Had Six Hours.

March 26, 2025: a counterfeit $WinLEW appeared. The warning went out six hours later — and it changed how this project proves who it is.

On March 26, 2025, someone created a counterfeit $WinLEW token.

Not a similar name. Not a parody. A deliberate impersonation, minted to be mistaken for this project's token by anyone who searched the name and did not check the address.

Six hours

Two timestamps tell that part of the story, and both can be checked without trusting us:

  • The counterfeit mint's first transaction on Solana: 2025-03-26T16:16:36Z.
  • The warning post on X: post id 1905021219067285813, which decodes to 2025-03-26T22:17:06Z.

Six hours and one minute. X post ids encode the instant they were created, so that second figure is not a claim about when we noticed — it is arithmetic on the post itself.

What the fake actually was

The counterfeit is GzMm4iuxoCU3NUAyvr1kxTbQY7U8SzEBP4F5vEQAvPoB. The real $WinLEW is, and has always been, DnrcdQVH7fdbmm4EyD7LjT9mNNozF5HuWMeKcpvjpump.

Read off the chain today:

  • Total supply 2,818,962.425 — it did not even imitate the real token's supply.
  • Mint authority revoked, no freeze authority — dressed to pass a quick safety check.
  • 449 transactions in its entire life, the last on 2025-12-20. It has been inert since.
  • 13 token accounts ever existed. 8 wallets still hold a balance.

That last line is the part worth sitting with. Eight wallets are still holding a counterfeit token that will never be worth anything, and some of those people probably still believe they own $WinLEW.

What it changed

The response was not to post louder. It was to make the real token checkable in the places people actually look — registered and verified across the platforms a buyer checks before buying, so that "which one is real" stops being a judgement call and becomes a lookup.

That is the reason this site is built the way it is. The burn ledger, the transparency centre, the snapshot tooling, the refusal to publish a number that cannot be traced to a transaction — all of it descends from a day when the difference between the real thing and a fake one was a string of characters most people would never compare.

What it cost

Here the honest answer is that the chain cannot settle it, so this is the project's own reading rather than a proven fact: the counterfeit landed at the worst possible moment, during the push that the March and April 2025 giveaways were built around, and the momentum did not recover.

Several giveaways from that period were tied to milestones — a follower count, a holder count — and those milestones were never reached. Which means the giveaways never triggered.

Nothing is owed. A giveaway conditioned on a milestone that did not happen is not a debt, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of dishonesty. Those records stay published exactly as they were written, with their conditions and their status intact, because deleting an unfulfilled promise is worse than leaving it standing unmet.

The December 7, 2026 airdrop exists to close that chapter deliberately rather than let it hang — not to settle a debt, because there is not one, but because a project that keeps a list of things that never happened should eventually do something instead.

The line that stuck

"Scammers don't just go after any crypto. They target the ones where they see the potential of $. This tells me #WinLEW is in the correct path to a successful future."

— Jim (@jim_xyo), the same day.

That is a generous way to read being counterfeited, and it is not the whole truth — being targeted is not an achievement, and it cost this project more than it gave. But there is something in it. Nobody bothers to forge a thing that nobody wants.

If you take one thing from this

Check the contract address. Every time. Not the name, not the logo, not the ticker, not who posted the link — all four of those were copied. The address is the only part that cannot be.

The real one is DnrcdQVH7fdbmm4EyD7LjT9mNNozF5HuWMeKcpvjpump. Verify it on RugCheck, on Solscan, on any explorer you like. Do not take our word for it — that is the entire point.
