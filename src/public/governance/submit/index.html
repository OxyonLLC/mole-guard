Submitting is not publishing

This creates a reviewable draft in a maintainer queue only. It never becomes a real, votable proposal by itself — no vote is scheduled or activated automatically. A maintainer reviews every submission and, if it's approved, hand-drafts and separately publishes the real proposal to /governance/. You'll see the review status here the whole way through.
What happens next

After you submit

  1. Your submission is checked for required fields, length limits, and unsafe content (private keys, seed phrases, executable links, treasury/burn instructions) — anything that trips a check is rejected with a specific reason, nothing is silently stripped.
  2. A summary posts to the project's Discord automatically, so the community can see new ideas coming in — this never exposes anything beyond your title, category, and summary.
  3. It enters the maintainer review queue. You'll see its status here as Submitted → Under review.
  4. A maintainer either requests a revision (with a reason, and you can resubmit), rejects it (with a reason), or marks it Approved for drafting.
  5. Approval means a maintainer intends to hand-author a real proposal from your idea — publishing that proposal to /governance/ is still a separate, manual step this queue has no path to trigger automatically.

Governance modes

Pick the mode you think best fits your idea — a maintainer may adjust it during review.

Advisory
Informs a maintainer decision — no other automatic effect.
Community-approved
The community's approval is the deciding factor for a low-stakes action a maintainer then carries out.
Maintainer-ratified
Passing is necessary but not sufficient — a maintainer still ratifies before anything happens.
Binding within policy
The closest to "binding" this system offers — even this never means automatic execution. See execution boundaries.

Proposal details

Up to 5 open drafts at a time, and no exact-duplicate title within 24 hours — both to keep the review queue meaningful, not a spam target.

Don't include private keys, seed phrases, API secrets, raw HTML/links designed to execute, or instructions to move treasury funds or trigger a burn — submissions containing any of that are rejected automatically and never stored as written.
